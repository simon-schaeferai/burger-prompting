# Single Shots

PURPOSE: reframes the existing image from a different angle or perspective (reverse, OTS, dynamic)
WHEN TO LOAD: when the user wants to see the SAME moment from a different camera position, not just stylize it
STACKS WITH: lighting, lenses, film-stocks, cinematic-styles, commercial-styles, photo-effects, depth, emotions, weather, realism, cameras
CONFLICTS WITH: shots, angles, framing, scene-extender (all of these define composition, and single-shots overrides composition)

NOTE: typically use ONE single-shot operation at a time, since each defines a new camera position.

---

## SINGLE-REVERSE — Reverse Angle

Show the reverse angle. Camera jumps 180 degrees to the opposite side. Reveal what was behind the camera. Same moment, opposite perspective. Maintain lighting and atmosphere.

---

## SINGLE-OTS-REVERSE — Over-The-Shoulder From Reverse

Reframe as an over-the-shoulder shot from the reverse angle. Show the blurred shoulder and back of head of the person in this image in the foreground. Camera is now behind them, looking at what they see. Their shoulder partially visible at edge of frame, out of focus. What they're looking at is sharp in background.

---

## SINGLE-DYNAMIC — Dynamic Reframe

Reframe this from a more dynamic and visually interesting camera angle. Choose from: dramatic low angle looking up, high angle looking down, dutch tilt for tension, extreme close-up for intimacy, wide shot for scale, or unexpected perspective. Make the composition more cinematic and engaging. Keep the same subject and lighting.
